QWhy do we say "Kyrie eleison" so many times in the liturgy?
"Kyrie eleison" is Greek for "Lord, have mercy." The repetition is intentional — it is not mere redundancy but an expression of continuous dependence on God's mercy. In the Coptic tradition, the 41-fold Kyrie during the liturgy corresponds to specific theological meanings, including the 41 years of Christ's earthly life (by Coptic reckoning). Repetition in liturgy is the language of the heart, not the intellect.

QWhat is the difference between the Liturgy of St. Basil and the Liturgy of St. Gregory?
The Liturgy of St. Basil is the standard Sunday liturgy and is used on most feast days. The Liturgy of St. Gregory is more elaborate and poetic — it is used only on the highest feasts: Christmas, Epiphany, and Easter Sunday. The Liturgy of St. Cyril (also called the Liturgy of St. Mark) is the oldest and is rarely used today, reserved for special occasions.
