Notes

Lessons, reflections, and studies compiled over time.

This lesson was taught to the 7th and 8th grade Sunday school class. The goal was to show students that chastity is not just a rule, but a way of life modeled by real people who struggled and won through faith.

St. Moses the Strong — Originally a violent criminal, Moses had a dramatic conversion and became one of the greatest Desert Fathers. His story shows that no one is beyond God's mercy, and that strength once directed at sin can be transformed toward holiness.

St. Mary of Egypt — A woman who spent years in sinful living, she experienced a miraculous call to repentance at the Church of the Holy Sepulchre. She spent the rest of her life in the desert in prayer and fasting.

The Prophet Joseph — When tempted by Potiphar's wife, he fled and said: "How then can I do this great wickedness, and sin against God?" (Gen. 39:9). He kept his purity at the cost of his freedom — and God honored him greatly.

Key lesson: Chastity is not weakness. It is the strength to say no to the world and yes to God.

St. Athanasius of Alexandria (c. 296–373 AD) is one of the greatest theologians in Christian history. His Latin title is Athanasius contra mundum — Athanasius against the world.

At the Council of Nicaea in 325 AD, the Arian heresy claimed that the Son of God was a created being. Athanasius argued passionately for homoousios — of the same substance — to describe the Son's relationship to the Father.

He was exiled five times by four different Roman emperors. His most important work, On the Incarnation, explains why God became man: "He became what we are, that He might make us what He is."

The Oghnostos (Reader) is a rank in the Minor Orders of the Coptic Orthodox Church diaconate. The word comes from the Greek anagnostes, meaning one who reads.

Liturgical role — The Reader proclaims the Epistle, the Praxis (Acts), and selected Psalms during the Divine Liturgy in Coptic, Arabic, or English depending on the congregation.

Spiritual calling — The Reader must prepare in advance, understand what he proclaims, and live in a manner worthy of the altar.

St. Macarius the Great (c. 300–391 AD) is one of the most revered Desert Fathers and a pillar of Coptic spirituality, commemorated on Parmoute 27.

Born in Upper Egypt, he became a disciple of St. Anthony the Great and settled in Wadi El Natrun, where the Macarian Monasteries still stand today.

His famous saying: "If you are quiet, you will have peace wherever you live." His Macarian Homilies deeply influenced Eastern Christian spirituality.

The Liturgy of St. Basil is the primary Eucharistic liturgy of the Coptic Orthodox Church, used on most Sundays and feast days throughout the year.

The Liturgy of the Word — Readings from the Pauline Epistle, Catholic Epistle, Acts, and Holy Gospel, each proclaimed by different orders of the diaconate.

The Anaphora — The central prayer of consecration in which the bread and wine become the Body and Blood of Christ.

Holy Communion — The faithful receive the Body from the priest and the Blood from the deacon via chalice.
