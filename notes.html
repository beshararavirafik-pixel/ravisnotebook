Lessons, reflections, and studies compiled over time.

This lesson was taught to the 7th and 8th grade Sunday school class. Chastity is not just a rule, but a way of life modeled by people who struggled and won through faith.

St. Moses the Strong - His dramatic conversion and becoming one of the greatest Desert Fathers shows no one is beyond God's mercy.

St. Mary of Egypt - She experienced a miraculous call to repentance at the Church of the Holy Sepulchre and spent the rest of her life in the desert.

The Prophet Joseph - When tempted, he fled and said: How then can I do this great wickedness, and sin against God? (Gen. 39:9). He kept his purity at the cost of his freedom.

St. Athanasius of Alexandria (c. 296-373 AD) is one of the greatest theologians in Christian history. His Latin title is Athanasius contra mundum.

At the Council of Nicaea in 325 AD he argued for homoousios - of the same substance - to describe the Son's relationship to the Father, against the Arian heresy.

He was exiled five times. His most important work, On the Incarnation: "He became what we are, that He might make us what He is."

The Oghnostos (Reader) is a rank in the Minor Orders of the Coptic Orthodox Church diaconate, from the Greek anagnostes, meaning one who reads.

Liturgical role - The Reader proclaims the Epistle, the Praxis (Acts), and selected Psalms during the Divine Liturgy.

Spiritual calling - The Reader must prepare in advance, understand what he proclaims, and live in a manner worthy of the altar.

St. Macarius the Great (c. 300-391 AD) is one of the most revered Desert Fathers, commemorated on Parmoute 27.

Born in Upper Egypt, he became a disciple of St. Anthony the Great and settled in Wadi El Natrun, where the Macarian Monasteries still stand today.

His famous saying: "If you are quiet, you will have peace wherever you live."

The Liturgy of St. Basil is the primary Eucharistic liturgy of the Coptic Orthodox Church, used on most Sundays and feast days.

The Liturgy of the Word - Readings from the Pauline Epistle, Catholic Epistle, Acts, and Holy Gospel.

The Anaphora - The central prayer of consecration in which the bread and wine become the Body and Blood of Christ.
